This assignment has four steps, as follows.

# Select a passage

Select a passage of about twelve lines from any of the works of Chaucer that we have read.
The passage should make grammatical sense on its own.
(Do not start or stop in the middle of a clause, for example.)

# Transcribe or copy the text

If you transcribe your passage from *The Oxford Chaucer* or another printed edition, disable automatic spelling correction in your word processor.
Check your transcription letter-by-letter.
If you copy and paste your passage from an on-line text of the *Canterbury Tales*, cite your source.
If your text derives from any source other than *The Oxford Chaucer*, cite your source.

# Translate

Translate the passage into Present Day English prose.
Your translation should express the meaning of the Middle English poem as precisely as possible, but in fluent modern English.
Do the translation yourself, using editorial word-glosses and dictionaries.
Any recent LLM can translate Middle English, but that won't help you read the language.

Here is an example of an acceptable translation of the opening of the Knight's Tale:

> Once, as old stories tell us, there was a duke named Theseus.
He was lord and governor of Athens and in his time he was such a great conqueror that there was none greater under the sun.

For comparison, here is the passage as printed in @LawtonNortonChaucer2019:

> | Whilom, as olde stories tellen us,
> | Ther was a duc that highte Theseus:
> | Of Atthenes he was lord and governour,
> | And in his time swich a conquerour
> | That gretter was there noon under the sonne.
